Working positioning: **We study how a known symbolic intervention can be realized in frozen text embeddings, comparing semantic-target editors with distribution-based transports.** This frames the investigation, not a priority or superiority claim. ## Draft section

**Fair representation learning.** Sensitive information can remain in text representations after explicit indicators are removed, including in occupational classification ([De-Arteaga et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09451)). Fair representation learning balances utility with restrictions on such information ([Zemel et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/zemel13.html); [Madras et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/madras18a.html)), while [Creager et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/creager19a.html) allow fairness requirements to change at test time. We focus on translating a known symbolic operation into a change of a pre-trained text representation, rather than removing sensitive information alone.

**Counterfactual fairness and text benchmarks.** Counterfactual fairness rests on causal and normative assumptions ([Counterfactual Fairness](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/a486cd07e4ac3d270571622f4f316ec5-Paper.pdf); [Causal Fairness Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11385)). [fairadapt](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v110i04) uses causal assumptions for data preprocessing. For text, [CEBaB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14140) provides human-edited concept changes, whereas [LIBERTy](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.399/) combines structural interventions with language-model-generated texts. We use LIBERTy to evaluate latent counterparts of a known symbolic operation. This benchmark provides a reference for evaluation, but does not establish real-world causal validity or downstream fairness.

**Semantic interfaces and causal abstraction.** Concept-based interfaces expose concepts within predictors, including post-hoc interfaces to pre-trained networks ([Concept Bottleneck Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/koh20a.html); [Post-hoc Concept Bottleneck Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15480)). Causal-abstraction methods align symbolic variables with neural representations ([Geiger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997); [Distributed Alignment Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536)), while [Causal Proxy Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/wu23b.html) use factual and approximate counterfactual supervision to reproduce model behaviour. Our semantic reader also extracts a structured state, but does not constrain the downstream predictor to this bottleneck. Instead, a symbolic operation defines a target state which a rendering function writes back into the latent representation.

**Post-hoc representation editing.** Linear concept erasure suppresses linearly accessible information ([INLP](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.647/); [LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819)), while [Representation Surgery](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09631v7) studies affine steering and moment matching. [Laguna et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13544v3) optimize concept agreement and proximity for each edited representation. The recent [MUtE preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11253v1) links concept erasure to deterministic counterfactual transports through a common representation. In contrast to Laguna et al.'s per-instance optimization, our semantic-target editors learn reusable target-conditioned maps, with mixture weights supplied by the reader and symbolic operation. We compare residual and convex-gradient maps under the same objective.

**Latent text control.** For text, [LatentOps](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.1030/) composes classifier-guided controls through latent ODEs, [MATTE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/afda6bf3fb086eabbaf161ba1cec5a9a-Abstract-Conference.html) studies counterfactual generation with content–style identification under relative sparsity, and [Lemberger and Saillenfest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00711) construct counterfactual representations for classifier explanations. We compare five latent renderers for an external multivariate symbolic operation on frozen document embeddings, fitting them without paired counterfactual targets and evaluating their recovery on held-out pairs.

**Deep counterfactual inference.** [Deep Structural Causal Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.06485) support abduction with normalizing flows and variational inference. [StyleFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.02401) edits StyleGAN representations by inverting under source attributes and rendering under changed attributes with the same reference code. [FairFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16431) uses invertible word-representation manipulation for counterfactual text augmentation. Our state-conditioned flow applies this shared-reference construction to document embeddings, fitting factual conditional likelihoods while taking target states from an external symbolic operation.

**Optimal transport.** Optimal transport selects correspondences through a geometric cost. [De Lara et al.](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v25/21-1440.html) connect transport-based and structural counterfactuals under additional assumptions. Neural OT approaches include convex-potential maps ([Makkuva et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/makkuva20a.html)), used for unpaired perturbation populations in [CellOT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-023-01969-x), and flow matching with OT-based paths ([Lipman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747); [Tong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00482)). Our OT-structured semantic editor uses a convex-gradient map without fitting a target density. The OT flow learns state-conditioned reference transports; the Gaussian baseline gives a restricted analytic version. We compare these map classes under the same symbolic operation, without equating distribution matching with individual counterfactual recovery.

**Counterfactual identification.** Distributional fit alone does not identify individual counterfactuals ([Nasr-Esfahany and Kiciman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09031)). [Counterfactual Cocycles](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13844v4) constructs identity- and path-consistent transports, while recent [dynamic-OT work](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08294v2) investigates identification under additional restrictions for multivariate outcomes. We do not assume these results establish identification for our models. We therefore assess semantic consistency, held-out latent recovery and text realization separately, without claiming causal recovery beyond the benchmark.
