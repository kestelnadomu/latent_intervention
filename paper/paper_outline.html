Abstract

  • Problem: reward models and predictors on text exploit spurious correlations tied to sensitive attributes
  • Gap: existing debiasing approaches either (a) bake bias-specific constraints into the encoder (HSIC-based, Ng et al. (2025)) or (b) require full retraining per new attribute
  • Proposal: a LatPlan-inspired (Asai et al. 2022) modular neurosymbolic architecture for text where a frozen, general-purpose text VAE is paired with learned manipulators that mimic symbolic operators on tabular metadata
  • Contribution: four-phase pipeline (encoder → semantic decoder → neurosymbolic manipulator → counterfactual fairness mitigation) with interpretability, encoder reuse, and counterfactual explanation as first-class properties

Introduction

Motivation

  • Fairness in text-based prediction systems (hiring, content moderation, reward modeling) requires mitigating sensitive-attribute influence
  • Counterfactual fairness (Kusner et al. 2017) as the target criterion: predictions should be stable under interventions on sensitive attributes
  • Standard approaches either require invasive encoder training or lack interpretability

The LatPlan analogy

  • LatPlan (Asai et al. 2022) learns discrete latent states and symbolic action operators from images
  • Our work ports this neurosymbolic architecture to text, replacing planning with counterfactual fairness as the downstream objective
  • Key adaptation: continuous (or quantized) text latents instead of binary propositional symbols; learned manipulators instead of PDDL operators

Contributions

  • A four-phase modular architecture that decouples encoding from bias mitigation
  • A training objective for latent space manipulators / interventions using a frozen semantic decoder as a neurosymbolic consistency bridge
  • Empirical demonstration of the approach on synthetic and real-world datasets, showing competitive fairness-accuracy trade-offs
    • Empirical comparison against HSIC-based encoder-level debiasing (Ng et al. 2025)
  • Analysis of trade-offs between identifiability guarantees and modularity/interpretability

Background and Related Work

Text Variational Autoencoders

  • Foundational work: Bowman et al. (2016) on LSTM-based text VAEs; posterior collapse problem
  • Transformer-based VAEs: Optimus (Li et al. 2020) as the first pretrained LM-VAE
  • Modern frameworks: LangVAE (Carvalho et al. 2025) with KV-cache injection for frozen-decoder training
  • Advances on LlaMaVAE (Zhang et al. 2023), T5VQVAE (Zhang, Carvalho, et al. 2024), graph-induced VAEs (Zhang, Valentino, et al. 2024)
  • Latent-space manipulability challenges: latent vacancy problem (Xu et al. 2020)

Neurosymbolic Latent-space Reasoning

  • LatPlan original and JAIR version (Asai and Fukunaga 2018; Asai et al. 2022)
  • State AutoEncoder and Action Model Acquisition (AMA)
  • First-order extensions (Asai 2019)
  • Plausibility heuristics for latent plan validation (Takata and Fukunaga 2023)
  • Relation to our Phase 2: manipulator as a learned, metadata-consistent action operator

Counterfactual Fairness

  • Foundational formulation (Kusner et al. 2017)
  • Path-specific refinements (Chiappa 2019)
  • Latent-representation methods: CLAIRE (Ma et al. 2023), GCFN (Grari et al. 2023)
  • Fair latent space construction with frozen generators (Hong et al. 2024)
  • GAN-based latent de-biasing (Ramaswamy et al. 2021)

Controllable and Manipulable Text Generation

  • Attribute-conditioned VAEs (Hu et al. 2017)
  • LatentOps: composable operators via ODEs (Liu et al. 2023)
  • Unsupervised discovery of latent directions (Anonymous 2020)
  • Diffusion-based latent control: RegDiff (Vaeth et al. 2025)

Reward Model Debiasing via Causal Representation Learning

  • Debiasing with theoretical guarantees via HSIC and surrogate conditioning (Ng et al. 2025)
  • Counterfactual invariance framing (Veitch et al. 2021)
  • Information-theoretic approaches: DIR (Li et al. 2025)
  • Positioning: we target fairness rather than reward hacking, but share the causal-representation framing

Problem Formulation

Setting

  • Text input \(x\), ground-truth label \(y\), sensitive metadata \(m\) (tabular)
  • Prediction task \(\hat{y} = f(x)\) where spurious correlations between \(m\) and \(y\) can bias \(f\)
  • Counterfactual fairness requirement: \(\hat{y}(x) = \hat{y}(x^{\text{cf}})\) where \(x^{\text{cf}}\) is the counterfactual text with flipped sensitive attribute

The identifiability vs. modularity trade-off

  • HSIC-based approaches (Ng et al. 2025): bias-specific encoder training, formal identifiability (Theorems 1–2), but no encoder reuse
  • Our claim: for many applications, encoder reuse, interpretability, and counterfactual explanation outweigh formal identifiability
  • Discussion of when each trade-off is appropriate

Architectural desiderata

  • Encoder reuse across attributes and tasks
  • Interpretable manipulation operators
  • Counterfactual explanations as a built-in capability
  • Testable neurosymbolic consistency (bridge between symbolic metadata edits and latent-space operations)

Proposed Architecture

Overview of the four-phase pipeline

  • Phase 0: Pretrained text VAE (encoder + decoder)
  • Phase 1: Semantic decoder from latent to metadata
  • Phase 2: Neurosymbolic manipulator learning metadata-consistent latent operations
  • Phase 3: Downstream predictor
  • Phase 4: Counterfactual fairness mitigation using frozen manipulator
  • Figure: architecture diagram

Phase 0: Text VAE backbone

  • Choice of backbone: LangVAE (Carvalho et al. 2025) for modularity and frozen-decoder efficiency
  • Alternative: T5VQVAE (Zhang, Carvalho, et al. 2024) for discrete latents with tighter LatPlan analogy
  • Training corpus and preprocessing details

Phase 1: Semantic decoder

  • Architecture: lightweight MLP from frozen latent \(z\) to metadata \(\hat{m}\)
  • Training objective: reconstruction loss on metadata (regression/classification per column)
  • Role: establishes the neurosymbolic bridge; provides interpretability and a consistency target for Phase 2

Phase 2: Neurosymbolic manipulator

  • Input: latent \(z\) and symbolic metadata edit \(\Delta m\)
  • Output: manipulated latent \(z'\) such that semantic_decoder(\(z'\)) matches manipulated metadata
  • Architecture options: linear direction, MLP, conditional normalizing flow
  • Loss: consistency with symbolic manipulator + decoded-text plausibility + minimality of change
  • Relation to LatPlan AMA (Asai et al. 2022) and LatentOps plug-in operators (Liu et al. 2023)

Phase 3: Predictor

  • Trained on frozen latents or manipulated latents
  • Standard MLP or linear head

Phase 4: Counterfactual fairness mitigation

  • In-processing: consistency loss between \(\hat{y}(z)\) and \(\hat{y}(z')\) where \(z'\) is counterfactually manipulated
  • Closely related to CLAIRE (Ma et al. 2023) objective

Theoretical Considerations

What the manipulator approach gives up vs. HSIC

  • No identifiability guarantee in the sense of Ng et al. (2025) Theorems 1–2
  • Non-identifiability of VAE latents (Hyvärinen et al. 2023; Locatello et al. 2019) carries over to counterfactual queries
  • Discussion of residual correlation channels (bias leakage through unmanipulated dimensions)

What the manipulator approach adds

  • Modularity argument: attribute-per-manipulator composition
  • Interpretability via direct inspection of manipulator operations
  • Consistency check via semantic decoder loss (testable neurosymbolic bridge)
  • Counterfactual explanation as a capability, not just fairness as a predicate

Optional: partial identifiability analysis for the manipulator

  • Conditions under which the learned manipulator recovers the “true” counterfactual direction
  • Possible use of causal ordering assumptions (Kori et al. 2023)
  • Honest acknowledgement of where guarantees end

Experimental Setup

Datasets

  • Synthetic text data with controllable sensitive attributes (for sanity checks)
  • Real-world dataset(s): [TBD — options include Bios (De-Arteaga et al. 2019), Jigsaw Toxicity, resume data]

Baselines

  • Vanilla predictor on raw text embeddings
  • HSIC-based debiased encoder (CARD, Ng et al. (2025))
  • CLAIRE-style counterfactual augmentation (Ma et al. 2023)
  • Unsupervised latent directions via PCA (Anonymous 2020)
  • LatentOps plug-in operator (Liu et al. 2023)

Evaluation metrics

  • Predictive accuracy and worst-case subgroup accuracy
  • Counterfactual fairness metric (prediction invariance on manipulated inputs)
  • Bias@C (Zhou et al. 2024) for concept bias
  • Disentanglement metrics from LangSpace (Carvalho et al. 2025): MIG, DCI, z-diff
  • Interpretability probes: linear probe recovery of metadata from manipulated vs. unmanipulated latents
  • Counterfactual explanation quality (human or automated eval)

Ablations

  • Effect of semantic decoder supervision on manipulator quality
  • Continuous vs. discrete (VQ) latents
  • Linear vs. nonlinear manipulator
  • Encoder finetuning vs. frozen

Discussion

When to use HSIC vs. manipulator

  • Decision framework: known single bias + willingness to retrain encoder → HSIC; multiple/evolving biases + need for explanation → manipulator
  • Hybrid pipelines: HSIC for known axes, manipulator for long tail

Connection to broader neurosymbolic AI

  • LatPlan’s vision for symbol grounding (Asai et al. 2022)
  • Our work as evidence that the LatPlan pattern ports beyond image-based planning
  • Open question: can we extract genuinely symbolic (PDDL-like) operators from the learned manipulator?

Limitations

  • No formal identifiability guarantee
  • Text VAE reconstruction quality as an upper bound on counterfactual text quality
  • Dependence on quality and coverage of metadata annotations

Conclusion

  • Summary of contributions
  • A modular, interpretable, LatPlan-inspired approach to text-based counterfactual fairness
  • Call for further work on partial identifiability guarantees for manipulator-based methods
